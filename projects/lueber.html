Core Sand Batch Mixer

Developed and validated a new horizontal batch mixer for core sand preparation, with focus on mixing performance, structural design, wear resistance, maintainability and compact integration.

Field

Mechanical engineering / Foundry equipment

Period

2016 – 2020

Focus

Product development / Mechanical design / Testing & optimization

Overview

Developing a new horizontal batch mixer for core sand preparation.

During my mechanical engineering studies, I worked part-time at Lüber and was responsible for the mechanical development of a new batch mixer for core sand preparation. The goal was to develop a high-performance mixer based on the company’s existing process knowledge and an evaluation of available mixer concepts.

The existing Lüber mixers used a vertical mixing shaft. This concept provided a large mixing volume and simplified the sealing of the shaft, but also had limitations in mixing time, discharge behaviour and maintenance access. With a vertical mixing axis, gravity continuously moves the sand towards the bottom of the vessel instead of supporting the circulation of the material.

A horizontal mixing axis was therefore investigated as a new approach. The objective was to use gravity to support the mixing and discharge process while achieving the required capacity in the same or a smaller installation space.

My work covered requirements definition, concept development and evaluation, complete mechanical design, FEM analysis, prototype construction and internal testing with sand. The design was subsequently optimized in several iterations for mixing performance, structural stiffness, wear resistance, sealing, manufacturing cost and maintainability.

Key challenges

High mixing performance in a compact and mechanically robust design.

The mixer had to process large batches within a short cycle time while remaining compact, reliable and easy to maintain. The combination of abrasive sand, liquid binder, rotating components and dynamic loads resulted in several mechanical design challenges.

  • High throughput within the available installation space
  • Homogeneous mixing of sand and binder within a short mixing cycle
  • Development of a mixing tool for effective material circulation
  • Sufficient structural stiffness under static and dynamic loads
  • Control of vibrations generated by the rotating mixing system
  • FEM analysis of structural components and load paths
  • Wear-resistant and low-adhesion materials for abrasive sand and binder
  • Reliable shaft sealing against sand, binder and water during cleaning
  • Low residual sand volume after discharge
  • Good access for cleaning, inspection and replacement of wear parts
  • Design for manufacturing, assembly and cost
  • Modular interfaces for integration into different plant configurations

Solution

A horizontal mixing system with a rotatable vessel.

The developed concept combines a horizontal mixing shaft with a rotatable mixing vessel. The vessel has one main opening that is used for both filling and discharge. For filling, the vessel is rotated into a defined position so that sand and binder can enter from above. After mixing, the vessel rotates into the discharge position and the prepared sand exits through the same opening.

The horizontal mixing tool lifts and circulates the sand inside the vessel. Gravity then returns the material into the mixing zone, supporting continuous circulation of sand and binder. The geometry of the mixing tool was developed and refined during testing to achieve a homogeneous mixture within the required cycle time.

During discharge, the vessel position and the rotating mixing tool support the removal of the prepared sand. This reduces the amount of material remaining inside the mixer between batches and simplifies access to the mixing chamber for cleaning and maintenance.

The horizontal shaft arrangement introduced additional structural and dynamic requirements. Loads from the mixer drive, mixing tool and moving sand had to be transferred through a compact supporting structure while limiting deformation and vibration. FEM simulations were carried out to evaluate critical structural components, verify stiffness and identify areas requiring reinforcement or design changes.

Material selection was another important part of the design. Components in contact with the mixture are exposed to highly abrasive sand while the binder causes material to adhere to surfaces. Wear parts and contact surfaces were therefore selected and designed with both abrasion resistance and low adhesion in mind. The shaft sealing system also had to prevent sand and binder from reaching the bearing area while remaining resistant to water-jet cleaning.

The first prototype was manufactured and tested internally with sand to verify the basic mechanical functions and mixing process. Test results were used for a second design iteration with improvements to the mixing tool, materials, structural design, manufacturing cost and maintenance access.

The final iteration was designed as a modular mixer subsystem. Standardized mechanical interfaces allowed the mixer to be installed on different platforms and transport systems. This provided the flexibility required for integration into customer-specific core sand preparation plants.

Reflection

What I took from the experience.

This project was one of my first opportunities to take responsibility for the development of a complete mechanical system. I worked from initial requirements and concepts through detailed design, simulation, prototype manufacturing and physical testing.

The development showed me the importance of understanding the physical process before designing the machine around it. The orientation of the mixing axis influenced mixing behaviour, discharge, sealing, structural loads and maintenance. These aspects therefore had to be considered together rather than as separate design tasks.

FEM analysis provided a useful method for evaluating structural stiffness and critical load paths before manufacturing. Prototype testing then complemented the simulation by showing the actual vibration behaviour, material flow, wear, sealing performance and residual sand inside the vessel.

The iterative development process was particularly valuable. Results from each prototype were transferred directly into the next design, allowing the mixer to be improved not only in performance but also in manufacturing, assembly, cleaning and maintenance.

Overall, the project strengthened my understanding of mechanical product development and the importance of combining calculation, simulation and physical testing when developing industrial machinery.
